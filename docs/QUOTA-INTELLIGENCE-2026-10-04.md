# Quota intelligence — quotas in words, from a sheet, and watched in the field

*Research-intelligence brief, Phase 4 ("Quota intelligence and Excel/CSV quota import"). Built 2026-10-04 on the Intelligent copilot (INTELLIGENT-COPILOT.md), the Quota dashboard (QUOTA-DASHBOARD.md) and the import engine (SURVEY-IMPORT-ENGINE.md).*

## What it does

Quotas were already enforced end to end — `def.quotas` with cells and limits, a `quota_check` in the flow, `quota_counts` incremented on completes, the Quota dashboard with its states, recount and audit — but they were set up by hand, cell by cell, and the copilot could create one only in the simplest shape. Now:

- **A quota in words.** "500 completes, 50/50 gender, interlocked with 18–34 (40%), 35–54 (35%) and 55+" is one `create_quota` with `dimensions`: each dimension a question, its bands option codes or labels (default: every option, equal shares) or numeric ranges, with optional shares. The engine builds the crossing on the real option codes, splits the total by largest remainder so the limits add up exactly, and places the quota check right after the last question the cells read. Explicit `cells` (a condition in the survey's logic language, a count or a percent of the total) work as before, and the two can mix. Soft quotas flag; hard quotas terminate or redirect to a URL.
- **The rest of the lifecycle as actions.** `update_quota` (rename, a new total that rescales every count cell in proportion, mode, what happens when full — copied onto the quota's check — count status, cells by label), `add_quota_cells` (the check moves if the new cells read a later question), `remove_quota_cells` and `delete_quota` (destructive, confirmed; the check and List Fill references go with it), `set_quota_check` (after a question or a block, with an optional condition; a placement before a question the quota reads is said).
- **Feasibility review.** `reviewQuotas` — in the survey review under its own category, in the Quotas tab, and in the copilot's outline on a quota turn — finds the groups no cell covers (every answer combination of the questions the cells read is tried, with band edges and their neighbours for numeric questions: "1 of 3 combinations falls outside every cell (Prefer not to say)"), cells that match the same respondent (one respondent counts in each — intended for flat dimensions, said for interlocked ones), count limits that do not add up to the total (over: a suggestion; under: a warning, with the rescale as the fix), percent cells under 100 or without a total, a cell at 0 (unlimited, not closed), a quota nothing checks, and a check that runs before its question (critical, with the move as the fix). It complements `lintStructure`'s shape checks rather than repeating them.
- **Fieldwork advice from the live counts.** `quotaAdvice(def, counts)`: a full cell while the rest is open (those respondents are being turned away — raise it, or make the quota soft), a cell filling well below its share of what has come in (with the projected count when the quota fills and the shortfall — boost, lower, or weight), nearly full, complete. Each adjustment is an `update_quota` the researcher previews in Changes and applies; nothing is rewritten silently. The copilot reads the same counts and advice on a quota turn, so "which cells are behind?" is answered from numbers.
- **A quota sheet imports.** The client's Excel or CSV is read into quotas in three layouts — LONG (dimension columns, a target column, Quota/Group and Label columns, a Total row), MATRIX (a cross-tab with the row dimension down and the column dimension across, Total rows and columns ignored), LIST (label + number) — then mapped against the survey as it is open: each header to a question (by code, variable, text, and decisively by the values under it: "Age" with 18-24 / 25-34 goes to the age-band question, with ranges to the numeric age), each value to an option (or several joined with /) or a numeric range ("18–24", "55+", "under 18", "65 and over"), each target to a count or a percent. What does not match is reported by row and left out, never approximated; the result is a proposal in Changes.
- **One quota system.** Every action writes `def.quotas` and the `quota_check` nodes the dashboard, the runtime and the List Fill already read; the counts stay in `quota_counts` and come through the dashboard's own route.

## Principles kept

- **Reasoning is separated from execution.** The model says "50/50 gender, three age bands"; the engine builds cells on real codes, adds limits up, places the check, and refuses an option that does not exist, a share over 100, a cell without a limit. The sheet reader guesses nothing: an unmatched header or value is an issue by row.
- **Human in the loop.** Every quota — from words, from the engine's advice, from a sheet — is a proposal in Changes; removing cells or a quota is destructive and confirmed; limits are never changed from the counts without the researcher applying the change.
- **Nothing duplicated.** One `Quota`, one `quota_check`, one `quota_counts`, one dashboard route; the Quota dashboard stays the place to edit by hand.

## Not done

- **The advice is not a pacing model.** It reads the counts as they stand (share of what has come in against share of the maximum); it does not know the field dates or the sample delivered, so "at this pace" means "at this mix".
- **Quota definitions in a Decipher quota sheet** are still reported, not read — the sheet reader here takes a plain target table, not Decipher's sheet format.
- **`Quota.onFull.message`** is still not shown by the runtime (it shows the interface's quota-full text); the action stores it for when it is.
- Phases 5–6 of the brief (automated analysis after fieldwork, reporting) are next.
