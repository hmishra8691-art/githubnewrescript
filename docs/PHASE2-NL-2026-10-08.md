# Research Engine — Phase 2: Natural-language intelligence (08-10-2026)

**Source:** the Research Engine audit (`RESEARCH-ENGINE-AUDIT-2026-10-08.md`, section K, Phase 2), on top of Phase 1. The outcome it set: *the ten example sentences each produce a plan, a question, or a proposal — never a dead end.* Every change below has a regression test and the tests were mutation-checked. Nothing existing was removed; the grammar, the recognisers and the model path behave as before for every sentence they already read.

## What was built

**Semantic object resolution (`packages/engine/src/nlSemantics.ts`)** — four deterministic resolvers over the survey, each saying how it resolved:

- `resolvePopulation`: *respondents under 25*, *people aged 18 to 24*, *anyone over 65*, *those 18+*, *under-25s*, *women*, *male respondents*, *those who switched*, *people who have not switched brands*, *Brand A users*, *those who prefer Brand B*, *those in the North*, and compounds (*under 18 and anyone over 65* → either). An age phrase finds the age question (numeric, or banded single-select); on bands, whole bands inside the range are selected and a band that straddles the bound is refused with the exact fix. The sentence's own comparator is kept on numeric ages (`< 25`, `> 65`, `>= 18 AND <= 65`). Two questions that fit are returned with the alternative so the recogniser asks.
- `resolveConcept`: a code or variable first, then a construct of that name and the questions that measure it, a question tagged with it, then the wording — ranked by how many of the phrase's words (as everyday families: *purchase/buy*, *intent/likely*, *age/old*…) it carries; equal candidates are `ambiguous`.
- `resolveRole`: *the screener* (screening role, a terminating question, a block titled like one, else the first page), *demographics* (segmentation role, open text excluded), *this question*, *the whole survey*, a block by title.
- `parseScale`: *5-point*, *seven point likert*, *1 to 10*, *0–10 where 0 = not at all, 10 = extremely*, *agree–disagree*, *satisfaction*, *likelihood*, *importance*, *quality*, *frequency*, *NPS*, *yes/no* → points, start and anchors (full label sets at 5 and 7 points).

The concept-word families moved here from the `measures` recogniser, which now imports them, so "which questions measure X" and "cross-tab X by Y" read a concept the same way.

**Recognisers (`nlIntent.ts`)** — `screenerEdit`, `scaleChange`, `crosstabs`, `keyCrosstabs`, `unconnected`, `objective`; the sentences are listed above. Each lands on the existing actions through the existing gate (`act` applies them to a copy first), says what it understood, refuses with the fix when the type cannot take it (a multi-select has no scale → the single-select conversion is offered with its actions), says "already so" when the rule or scale is there, and asks with one executable choice per candidate when a phrase fits several questions.

**What the engine can do without a model** — a `model` interpretation now carries `fallback` (a question and executable choices) for "add a question to measure *X*" (a small library of standard items: purchase intent, satisfaction, NPS, awareness, importance, loyalty, trust, usage frequency, price perception) and "create a research design for *Y*" (record the objective). The Studio uses it in three places: on a failed model turn (the "Without a model" block under the failure), when the Studio already knows there is no model (an engine question, no call made), and after an unusable reply (the failed card with the choices stands instead of the grammar's guess at a sentence the engine declined to read).

**The change plan (`apps/studio/lib/copilot/changePlan.ts`, the turn route, `useCopilot`, `CopilotCard`)** — a generation is planned before it is built. The route's plan stage asks for a schema-bound plan (`kind: "plan"`, items with title, kind, objects, reason, detail; the model's questions and assumptions), no actions. The card shows it as *CHANGE PLAN*: items ticked by default, Build or Set aside. Building sends the approved items back (`stage: "execute"`); each is built with its own schema-bound call that names the item, the whole plan and what earlier items produced; the replies merge into one proposal that goes through the same gate, review and Apply. An item that fails (cut off, refused) is reported on the built card and the rest still land. "Plan first" is a toggle in the Intelligent header (on by default, kept per browser); off, the single-reply turn runs as before. The sandbox's scripted replies keep working: a reply that is not plan-shaped falls through to the direct turn, so every existing suite passes unchanged.

Mutation checks: engine semantics and recognisers 36 mutations, all caught after three tests were strengthened and two redundant guards removed (the concept tiers simplified; an unreachable deferral check dropped); Studio surfaces 13 mutations through the browser suite, all caught after one check was strengthened (plan first off with a plan-shaped answer).

Regression: the full corpus (130 suites) passes except the 10 known baseline failures (4 need Postgres, 6 unchanged from the previous runs). Two suites were adjusted for behaviour Phase 2 changed on purpose: `phase1-reliability-test.mjs` used the screener sentence as its example of a model hand-off, which the engine now reads itself (it uses a rewording now); `analysis-framework-test.mjs` exposed a compound sentence (“add a crosstab of X by Y and test …”) that the new crosstab recogniser swallowed — it now leaves a sentence with a second instruction to the model, with a unit test. `pnpm -r test` is green across 17 packages; both typechecks and the auth-guard audit are clean.
